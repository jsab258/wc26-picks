## What it is, in his words

Every system a tile, grouped by area, coloured by status, one screen,
phone-first, tap a tile for status, blocker and decisions. IT SITS BESIDE THE
GLANCE, NOT INSIDE IT: the glance is today, the map is the whole. So it is a
second page, linked from the glance and linking back, and it does not add a
sixth block to the five the glance is allowed.

## The renderer holds no facts of its own

Every word on the page comes from the inventory file. If a tile shows a status,
the data said so; if a tile has no blocker, the data said `none`. A renderer
that fills a gap with a plausible default is how a map ends up describing a
project nobody has.

The count is the guard: `tiles=N` printed beside `entries=N`. They can only
differ if the renderer dropped or invented one, and both are worth catching.

## Colour is a scale with a named meaning

Three status values, three colours, and the key is ON THE PAGE. A colour scheme
a reader has to remember is a colour scheme that gets misread on a phone in a
hurry, and the whole point of the map is a glance at the whole shape.

## Both halves, accepting first

Accepting: render the live inventory, print the counts and the per-area
breakdown, then open the page and say what is on it. A breakdown per area is
the axis the data actually varies on, which is the standing pattern: eight
blocks once hung over open sea at a foot gap of 0.00 exactly, and the per-edge
breakdown is the half that saw it.

Rejecting, two planted cases: a data file with one entry whose area is not one
of the five, which must be refused by name; and an empty data file, which must
render "nothing measured" rather than a clean empty page that reads as a
project with no systems.
