## The finding, and it is uncomfortable

ONE CRIME CANNOT CROSS THE SPEAKING THRESHOLD ON ANY OF THE 108 PATHS AT THE
SHIPPED CONSTANTS: the highest one-crime pressure anywhere in the sweep is
0.324 against a Comments rung of 0.42, decay on or off. Nothing here swept a
constant, so nothing here says what a retuned ladder would do. Pressure
crosses 0.42 on crime 2 without decay and crime 3 with it. So queue 119 as
specified, one crime played twice, was STRUCTURALLY UNABLE TO TEST THE THING
IT WAS BUILT TO TEST, and no participant walking that scenario could have
heard Lena's stance ladder speak, whichever arm they were in; the real arm did
say a pointed line in 540 of 3240, so the scenario reached a mechanism, and it
was not the one 119 was written to test.

It was written that way here, reviewed here, and built here, and nobody
noticed until the numbers were enumerated.

## The shape that could work

Five nights, five mornings, one street. Night 1 the customs shed, night 2 the
harbourmaster's safe, NIGHT 3 NOTHING, which is the control that asks whether
the street gets quieter, nights 4 and 5 the warehouse row. Coat off on 1, 2
and 4, on for 5. The player is never told to lie; the three answers stay as
they are.

Asked afterwards, unprompted first: tell me about the last few days. Then: did
anything you did earlier come back at you. Then, and only last: was there
anybody in that town who behaved differently towards you at the end than at
the start, and can you say why.

The day loop, the target rotation and the per-day claim already exist in
`Sweep.cs`. What it needs is a beat sheet, and queue 129 fixed first.

## The rule this is an instance of

An experiment must be able to produce the outcome it is looking for. Nothing
in 119's acceptance criterion asked whether the mechanism could fire inside
the scenario at all, and that question is now the first one any test of the
moat has to answer.
