## The residue queue 113 deliberately did not close

`Adjudicator.Binds` refuses a requirement that nobody in the declared range
could fail. It says nothing about a requirement almost nobody IN PLAY would
fail. `cash:1`, `hour:1` and `heat:99` all bind and barely constrain.

So the model no longer chooses WHETHER a requirement can refuse. IT STILL
CHOOSES HOW HARD. That is a smaller authority than the one 113 removed and it
is still authority.

## Why this was not fixed with 113, and the reason is the rule

A floor price is a BOUND, and rule 2 says never set a threshold you have not
measured. The builder was right to refuse to invent one under time pressure,
and the director was right to accept the refusal. Inventing "cash must be at
least 20" would have been a number with no evidence sitting in Core forever.

## What the series must contain

At the moment of adjudication, not at session start: `Clean`, `Dirty`, `Hour`,
`Heat`. And separately, the amounts the model actually names, per check.
Both halves matter: a floor is only meaningful as a relation between what
players have and what the model asks for.

Say which statistic each number is. A median amount and a peak wallet answer
different questions, and a floor derived from mismatched statistics is the
fault this project has recorded more than once.

## Both halves

Accepting: the series prints from real runs with its denominator, and a floor
derived from it refuses a formality while passing a real requirement.
Rejecting: a floor with no series behind it is refused by review. So is a
series taken from fixtures rather than play, because the question is what
players and models actually do.
