# Gap 3: household contents and upstairs possessions

STATUS: SPEC. Commission `atlas-02`, pinned to
`f3f395c5dda6be684183ef4f02c1d0a533207bbf`. Written 2026-09-08.

WHAT THIS IS. What is actually inside a British house between 1988 and 1992,
with the ownership rates that decide HOW MANY houses have it, plus the
upstairs, which is the half nobody researches and the half a burglary, a
search or a private conversation happens in.

WHY THE RATES MATTER MORE THAN THE LISTS. A prop list makes every house the
same house. A rate makes the town unequal, which is what it was: in 1991
nearly a fifth of households had no central heating and about half had no
microwave, so the difference between two front rooms is a fact about income
and not a decorating choice. Every number below is a dial an assembler can
turn.

Labels are as defined in `small-pub-plan-measured.md` section 0. The same
sourcing limit applies, and it bites hardest in this file: see HOLE 1.

## 1. The ownership dials, dated

- CITED: 1991 Census, England and Wales: 18.5 percent of households had NO
  central heating; 1.3 percent lacked or shared a bath or inside WC. Source:
  1991 Census profile for England and Wales,
  https://www.conwy.gov.uk/en/Council/Archived/Statistics-and-research/Census/Census-1991/Assets/documents-Comp-areas/England-Wales-1991-Census-profile.pdf .
- CITED: central heating rose from 37 percent of households in 1972 to 59
  percent in 1981 and 92 percent by 2000. Source: ONS General Lifestyle Survey
  overview, chapter 4, Housing and Consumer Durables,
  https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/personalandhouseholdfinances/incomeandwealth/compendium/generallifestylesurvey/2013-03-07/chapter4housingandconsumerdurablesgenerallifestylesurveyoverviewareportonthe2011generallifestylesurvey .
  This corroborates the census reading from a second direction, which is why
  central heating is the one dial in this file I would let a builder trust
  without further work.
- CITED: 55 percent of UK households contained a microwave oven by 1991.
  Source: Statista series on household microwave penetration in the UK,
  https://www.statista.com/statistics/289155/household-microwave-penetration-in-the-uk .
- CITED, WEAK: nearly 20 percent of UK households had a video recorder in 1983
  and 25 percent in early 1985. Source: 1980sactual.com. No 1990 figure was
  retrievable. DERIVED BOUND ONLY: VCR ownership in 1990 is well above 25
  percent and below universal; a common recollection is around two thirds, and
  that recollection is NOT sourced here and must not be printed as a number.
- CITED: 35 percent of households had a telephone in 1970. Source: Statista
  landline series, https://www.statista.com/statistics/289158/telephone-presence-in-households-in-the-uk .
  No 1990 value retrievable. HOLE.
- CITED: there were 92,000 phone boxes in the UK in 1992, their peak. Source:
  as above (search summary). DERIVED CONSEQUENCE, from 1992 being the peak of
  the box count and the window ending in 1992: the phone box is not a quaint
  survivor in this window, it is infrastructure at its maximum, and a house
  without a phone is a household whose calls happen in one.
- CITED: doorstep milk delivery fell from 45 percent of the retail milk market
  in 1995 to 3 percent today. Source: AHDB and House of Commons Library dairy
  statistics, https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn02721/ .
  DERIVED BOUND: in 1990 doorstep share is at or above 45 percent, so the milk
  float and the bottles on the step are a MAJORITY behaviour in the window,
  not nostalgia.
- CITED: the colour TV licence was 71 pounds in April 1990 and 77 pounds in
  April 1991. Source: TV Licence cost history,
  https://tvlicencecost.com/tv-licence-cost-history and
  https://www.terramedia.co.uk/reference/statistics/television/television_licence_fee.htm .
- CITED: average gross weekly earnings for full-time adults in Great Britain
  in 1990 were 263.10 pounds (men 295.60, women 201.50). Source: ONS, "Average
  salary between 1980 and 1997",
  https://www.ons.gov.uk/aboutus/transparencyandgovernance/freedomofinformationfoi/averagesalarybetween1980and1997 .
  DERIVED: the TV licence is about a quarter of one week's average pay, which
  is why the detector-van letter is a real fear in a poor house and worth a
  prop.
- HOLE 1, and it is the big one: the General Household Survey and Social
  Trends tables that carry annual ownership rates for video recorders, home
  computers, telephones, tumble dryers, dishwashers, freezers and the CD
  player
  across 1988 to 1992 exist and are named, but every host that carries them is
  blocked to this session. Ten of the dials this file wants are therefore
  bounds and single points rather than a series. Named as the first research
  task in this gap.

## 2. Downstairs, room by room

Front room, and the rule is that this is the room the street is allowed to see
- Three-piece suite; a gas or coal fire, or a radiator in four houses out of
  five (CITED, section 1); a colour television with FOUR channels and teletext
  (see section 4); a video recorder on or under it with a stack of rented and
  home-recorded tapes; a music centre or separate hi-fi with vinyl and
  cassettes, and a CD player only in a better-off house; a display cabinet;
  photographs; a wall unit.
- ASSUMED, and flagged: the specific furniture types above are period
  knowledge without a source in this file. Only the heating, the channel
  count and the licence are CITED.

Kitchen
- Gas or electric cooker, fridge, washing machine (twin tub only in the
  poorest or oldest household by 1990, ASSUMED), a microwave in about half of
  houses (CITED), a chest freezer in the outhouse or garage rather than the
  kitchen where the house is old.
- Formica or laminate worktops; a larder or a fitted run depending on the
  layer the house belongs to in `hillside-housing-dated-series.md`.

Hall
- The telephone lives HERE, on a table, in a house that has one: a wired
  landline with a cord, no mobile anywhere in the world of this game. An
  answering machine with a cassette in it is a middle-class or a
  business-adjacent object rather than a normal one (ASSUMED).
- The meter cupboard. CITED: coin, token, card and key prepayment meters were
  all in use in the UK across this era, and prepayment is distinguished from
  the quarterly bill paid in arrears. Source: Ofgem, "Prepayment Meters, A
  Consultation Document", 1999,
  https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/docs/1999/10/2414-ppoct_0.pdf .
  HOLE 2: I could not date the switchover from coin to token or key meters, so
  do not put a specific meter type in a specific year without more work. A
  coin meter with a shilling or 50p slot is the right SHAPE for a poor
  household in this window.

The doorstep economy, which is the reason a house has a hall table with
envelopes on it
- CITED: doorstep collectors, the "coupon man", walked street by street
  gathering football pools coupons and stakes; the weekly visit sat alongside
  the milkman, the insurance collector and the catalogue agent; the pools
  remained big business through the 1980s and into the 1990s, and the National
  Lottery of 1994 began their end. Source: Football Heritage, "Hopes, Draws
  and Dividends", https://www.footballheritage.co.uk/hopes-draws-and-dividends ;
  thegamehunter.co.uk, "Whatever Happened to the Football Pools?".
- DERIVED WHY IT IS WORTH HAVING, the four callers each cited above: four
  recurring callers at a front door in the window (milk, pools, insurance,
  catalogue). What each carries, a book, a bag and a route, is ASSUMED. That is
  a schedule the gossip simulation can eat, and it is dated.

## 3. Upstairs, which is where the private things are

The airing cupboard
- A lagged hot water cylinder with an immersion heater and a switch, shelves
  of towels and sheets above it. In a house with no central heating (about one
  in five, CITED) the cylinder is heated by the immersion or a back boiler.
  ASSUMED beyond the heating rate.

The beds
- CITED: the duvet reached Britain as the "continental quilt", sold from 1964
  and catching on through the 1970s, and it displaced sheets and blankets.
  Source: Soak and Sleep, "How the Duvet Came to Britain",
  https://www.soakandsleep.com/blog/post/how-the-duvet-came-to-britain .
  HOLE 3: no percentage for 1990, so the mix of duvet and blanket houses is
  ASSUMED. The safe reading is that the duvet is normal in a young household
  and blankets with a candlewick or eiderdown persist in an old one.

What is under the bed and in the drawers, which is the part a crime sim needs
- Documents, because this is a paper world: rent book, insurance book, savings
  book, betting slips, wage packets, a chequebook and a cheque guarantee card,
  premium bonds, the catalogue account card, letters. No printouts, no cards
  with chips, no PIN.
- Cash. CITED: the round one pound COIN entered circulation on 21 April 1983;
  the halfpenny left circulation in 1984; a new smaller 5p came in 1990 and
  the old large 5p and the shilling were demonetised at the end of that year;
  the 10p was reduced in 1992 and the old 10p and the florin demonetised.
  Source: Coins of the UK, https://www.coins-of-the-uk.co.uk/dec.html ; "One
  pound coin", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_pound_coin (search summary).
- CITED: the Bank of England pound NOTE was withdrawn in 1988; Series E notes
  arrived with the 5 pound from June 1990, the 20 pound from June 1991 and the
  10 pound from April 1992. Source: Bank of England, withdrawn banknotes,
  https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/banknotes/withdrawn-banknotes .
- DERIVED CONSEQUENCE, from the cited coin and note withdrawal dates, and a
  hard rule rather than a flourish: a purse in 1990 holds coins of two 5p sizes
  for part of the year and a 10p that is still large; it holds no pound note in England and no two pound coin. And because
  real banknotes carry real people's portraits, the world's notes are
  FICTIONAL by canon: research the real ones as evidence, print none of them.

Wardrobes and drawers
- See `adult-clothing-by-occupation.md` for what is in them, which is the same
  research from the other end.

The loft
- Hatch, water tank, glass fibre insulation, suitcases, decorations, the boxes
  a family cannot throw away. ASSUMED entirely; I found no source and it needs
  none, but it is labelled so nobody quotes this file as evidence.

## 4. The 1990 media stack, which dates a room faster than furniture does

- FOUR terrestrial channels: BBC1, BBC2, ITV and Channel 4, with teletext
  (Ceefax on the BBC, Oracle on ITV and Channel 4). Channel 5 does not exist
  until 1997. ASSUMED as common knowledge; the searches returned the licence
  fee but no source on the channel line-up, so it is labelled rather than
  cited. HOLE 4.
- CITED: satellite is new, contested and visible. Sky had a million dishes
  sold by late 1990, BSB launched in March 1990 and merged into Sky in
  November 1990. Source and detail in
  `hillside-housing-dated-series.md`, the 1988 to 1992 overlay.
- Formats present: VHS cassettes, audio cassettes, vinyl, CDs in a minority of
  homes, and no DVD, no MP3, no games console beyond the 8-bit and 16-bit
  machines of the period.
- WHAT MUST NOT BE IN SHOT, and this list is worth more than any prop list
  because it is checkable: no mobile handset, no cordless handset in a poor
  house, no networked computer service of any kind, no domestic CCTV, no flat panel display, no microwave
  with a digital clock in a house that has no microwave, no lined pint glass
  (see the pub file), no two pound coin, no small 10p before 1992, no wheelie
  bin until it can be dated (HOLE 5), no barcode-scanning till in a corner
  shop unless it is sourced.
