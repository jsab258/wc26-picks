# Gap 4: adult clothing by occupation

STATUS: SPEC, and the THINNEST of the five. Commission `atlas-02`, pinned to
`f3f395c5dda6be684183ef4f02c1d0a533207bbf`. Written 2026-09-08.

WHAT THIS IS. What an adult in a British port town wears to work between 1988
and 1992, by occupation, with the dated facts I could source and a plain
statement of how much of this gap I did not close.

READ THIS BEFORE USING THE FILE. Occupational clothing turned out to be the
hardest of the five gaps to source at a dated level. The trade standards are
documented but not dated in anything I could reach; the fisherfolk sources are
overwhelmingly Victorian; the fashion sources are picture-led and vague about
years. Sections 1 and 2 are solid and dated. Sections 3 to 6 are SHAPE, and
say so at the top of each. A builder who treats section 5 as researched is
using this file against its own warning label.

Labels are as defined in `small-pub-plan-measured.md` section 0. Same sourcing
limit.

## 1. Dockers, and the single most important date in this file

- CITED: the National Dock Labour Scheme was abolished on 3 July 1989. The
  government announced its intention on 6 April 1989; registered dock workers
  struck unofficially on 7 April; the national strike that followed was
  defeated, union recognition was broken in every port except Liverpool, wages
  were cut and part-time casual work was introduced. Sources: Bristol Radical
  History Group, "State Intervention and the Abolition of the National Dock
  Labour Scheme",
  https://www.brh.org.uk/site/articles/abolition-of-the-national-dock-labour-scheme/ ;
  Hansard, Dock Labour Scheme, 6 April 1989; labournet.net dockers archive.
- WHY IT BELONGS IN A CLOTHING FILE: because it decides who is wearing the
  clothes. A Meridian docker in 1988 is a registered man with a job for life;
  the same man in 1990 is casual labour, waiting at a gate, in the same coat.
  The wardrobe barely changes and the standing changes completely, and that is
  the kind of fact this project's social memory is built to use. It also dates
  the town's grievances precisely: a 1990 dockside conversation about the
  Scheme is a conversation about something that happened last year.
- CITED: the donkey jacket is a medium-length unlined jacket in black or dark
  blue thick Melton wool with the shoulders reinforced front and back in
  leather or PVC, developed in 1888 for navvies on the Manchester Ship Canal;
  the shoulder panel sheds rain and takes the weight of timber or tools
  carried on the shoulder; it was in wide use through the 1980s among dockers,
  builders, miners and binmen. Sources: "Donkey jacket",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_jacket (search summary); Howsafe,
  "Remembering the Donkey Jacket",
  https://www.howsafe.co.uk/blog/post/remembering-the-donkey-jacket-a-workwear-icon ;
  a dated example, "Original 1980s Donkey Jacket by Clares Workwear",
  https://www.blightymilitaria.com/en-GB/post-1979/original-1980s-duffle-coat-donkey-jacket-by-clares-workwear---size-l/prod_55300 .
- CITED: the Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992 require
  employers to provide suitable PPE, including footwear, where a risk
  assessment identifies a hazard. Source: search summary of UK safety-footwear
  guidance citing the 1992 Regulations. NOTE THE DATE: the Regulations are
  1992, at the very end of the window, so through most of 1988 to 1992 the
  dock is a place where protective kit is patchy and personal rather than
  issued and enforced. That is a visual fact: bare heads, a mix of boots, one
  man in wellingtons.
- HOLE 1: HI-VIS. I could not date when high-visibility clothing became normal
  on British docks. EN 471 appears in the sources only in its 2003 revision;
  the earlier British standards I searched for (BS 3547, BS 5867) returned
  nothing usable. DO NOT put a hi-vis vest on a 1990 docker on this file's
  authority. If hi-vis appears, it should be the exception, a supervisor or a
  crane banksman, and it should be flagged for verification.
- HOLE 2: the safety boot standards of the window (BS 1870 and its successor
  EN 345) could not be dated. Steel toecaps existed; which standard was marked
  in the boot is unknown here.

## 2. Police, and the window is entirely pre-reform

- CITED: for most of the twentieth century up to the mid-1990s a male officer
  wore a dark blue, almost black, tunic with polished silver buttons and
  trousers of matching colour WITH A SEWN-IN TRUNCHEON POCKET. Source:
  "Police uniforms and equipment in the United Kingdom",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_uniforms_and_equipment_in_the_United_Kingdom
  (search summary); Avon and Somerset Constabulary uniform history,
  http://avonsomersetpolice.blogspot.com/2015/07/uniform.html .
- CITED: the woollen NATO-style ribbed V-neck pullover in navy blue was
  introduced about 1986, so it is present and still newish in the window.
  Source: as above.
- CITED: until the mid-1990s most forces used a traditional 14 inch wooden
  truncheon; side-handled and ASP batons replaced it in the 1990s, with
  Bedfordshire Specials among the first issued the side-handled baton in 1995.
  Source: Avon and Somerset Constabulary equipment history,
  https://avonsomersetpolice.blogspot.com/2015/07/equipment-part-3-truncheons.html .
- CITED: the Home Office uniform change to black trousers, shirt, NATO jumper,
  stab vest, service belt and reflective jacket came in 1994. Source: as
  above. DERIVED CONSEQUENCE, from the 1994 change date and the mid-1990s
  truncheon line above: everything a player sees on a Meridian officer in 1988
  to 1992 is the OLD uniform. Custodian helmet on foot patrol, flat cap in a
  car, tunic, whistle chain, wooden truncheon in a trouser pocket, a personal
  radio, and NO stab vest, NO reflective jacket, NO baton on the belt. A
  modern-looking constable is the loudest period error available in this
  world and this is the paragraph that prevents it.
- CITED: male Special Constables were issued custodian helmets for the first
  time in 1992. Source: "Custodian helmet",
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custodian_helmet (search summary).

## 3. Fishermen. SHAPE ONLY, and the sources are Victorian

- CITED, but about the 1800s: the gansey or guernsey, a tightly knitted wool
  jumper with underarm gussets, side slits and short sleeves so cuffs cannot
  snag, knitted to family patterns; oilskin hat, trousers, jacket and coat in
  heavy linen or cotton coated with linseed oil; the sou'wester with a
  slanting brim, ear flaps and a chin strap; knee-high leather sea boots
  treated with oil. Sources: Lloyd's Register Foundation Heritage, "Guardians
  of the fishers: Ganseys, oilskins and PFDs",
  https://heritage.lrfoundation.org.uk/stories/guardians-of-the-fishers-ganseys-oilskins-and-pfds-in-fishing-culture ;
  Jayne Shrimpton, "History in the details: Fisherfolk Dress",
  https://www.thegenealogist.co.uk/featuredarticles/discover-your-ancestors/periodical/83/history-in-the-details-fisherfolk-dress-3506/ .
- HOLE 3, stated bluntly: I found NO source describing what a British
  trawlerman actually wore in 1990. By then the oilskin is PVC or PU coated
  fabric in yellow or orange, the boots are moulded rubber, and the gansey is
  a heritage object rather than working kit, but NONE of that is sourced here
  and a builder must not take it from this file. Dressing a 1990 crew in
  linseed oilskins and a hand-knitted gansey would be a museum error of about
  eighty years, and dressing them in modern branded offshore kit would be an
  error of thirty in the other direction.

## 4. Market traders and food trades. SHAPE ONLY

- CITED: stall holders wore an apron, often a butcher's apron, with a money
  bag or a multi-pocket money pouch at the waist; the greengrocer, fishmonger
  and baker were identifiable by a trade-specific apron, smock or jacket.
  Sources: Alamy documentary photograph captions,
  https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo/market-trader-wearing-apron.html ; The
  Kitchn on Old Town Clothing's small trades garments,
  https://www.thekitchn.com/how-to-dress-like-a-fishmonger-167927 .
- No date attaches to any of this, so it is shape. It is nonetheless robust:
  the apron and the money pouch are continuous across the century.

## 5. Office, professional and evening dress. SHAPE ONLY, and thin

- CITED: power dressing continued from the 1980s into the 1990s with tailored
  trouser suits for women, but the jacket softened and shoulder pads got much
  smaller as the 1990s began; the decade trended more minimalist. Source:
  Aberdeen City, "1990s Fashion", https://www.aberdeencity.gov.uk/node/7662 ;
  nostalgiacentral.com on shoulder pads.
- CITED: the nylon leisure suit of the period, a matching zip jacket and
  elasticated trouser with an outer nylon skin and colour panels, was the
  signature leisure garment of the late 1980s and early 1990s. Source: Blue17
  vintage, blog article on the garment, blue17.co.uk, searched 8 September
  2026, which is this commission's date and the date every search in this file
  was run.
  URL WITHHELD ON PURPOSE: the garment's common period name, which is also the
  slug of that article, is a token `tools/canon-gate.py` screens as a real
  brand, and the recorded studio answer to that class of collision is to
  reword rather than to loosen the gate. The garment is a matching nylon
  tracksuit; searching that host for it finds the article in one hit.
- HOLE 4: nothing dated on men's business dress in Britain in this window, on
  what a solicitor or a clerk in the Exchange wore, or on prices. The Exchange
  district's wardrobe is currently unresearched.

## 6. Nurses, and a caution

- CITED: white disposable paper caps replaced cotton ones in the 1970s;
  plastic aprons displaced traditional ones in the 1980s and outerwear began to
  disappear; cap and buckle styles differentiated positions in the hierarchy;
  staff nurses wear blue and white stripes with dark blue piping and belt.
  Sources: Royal College of Nursing, "Nurses' uniforms",
  https://rcn.epexio.com/guides/nurse-uniforms.pdf ; Scarlet Finders,
  "Identifying Uniform", http://www.scarletfinders.co.uk/150.html .
- CAUTION: the colour-by-rank scheme varied by hospital and trust and the
  sources above mix eras. Treat as shape.

## 7. What no source was found for at all

- Publican and bar staff dress. Nothing. This is an unfortunate hole given
  that Mickey's is the player's pub: what a licensee and a barmaid wore behind
  a bar in 1990 is currently unresearched and unassumed.
- Priests and ministers outside a service, teachers, bus and train crews'
  uniforms of the period, and dockworkers' union or company badges.
- Clothing PRICES anywhere in the window, which would have given the same
  income-inequality dial that the household file gets from ownership rates.
  The Argos catalogues named in `household-contents-and-upstairs.md` HOLE 6
  carry clothing pages as well and would close this at the same time.

## 8. The rule that binds anything made from this file

Canon: every brand is fictional and no real person appears. Research real
workwear, real uniforms and real catalogues as evidence, and put no real name,
logo or maker's mark on a garment in Meridian. Where a garment needs a mark,
that mark is the brand bible's to mint, not this file's.
