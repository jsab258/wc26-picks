## 1. The atlas row, exactly as it reads, and what survives it

`origin/art/atlas-01:production/art/atlas-01/DISTRICTS.md`, line 12, read
2026-09-10:

    refits; daytime cleaning/deliveries precede night queues

Five clauses. TWO ARE VOID under D17: the drink trade, and the wine-bar refits.
THREE SURVIVE AND ARE THE MATERIAL FOR EVERYTHING BELOW: the cinema, the taxi
calls, and the day-before-night rhythm.

AND HERE IS THE FINDING OF THE WHOLE EXERCISE, which came from re-reading
rather than from inventing: THE ATLAS ALREADY DESCRIBED A PARADE THAT WORKS
WITHOUT DRINK. Its four materials for this district are glazed tile, oxblood
vinyl, painted stucco and small neon; all four survive. Its four characteristic
objects are a queue rope, a cinema poster case, an ashtray and a TAXI LANDLINE
NOTICE; all four survive, tobacco not being covered by D17. Its one information
venue is a cinema. NOTHING IN THE ATLAS'S DATA IS TOUCHED BY THIS REDESIGN.
What changes is one clause of one row of prose.

## 2. The thesis

THE PARADE IS NOT WHERE PEOPLE DRINK. IT IS WHERE PEOPLE ARE TURNED OUT AND
HAVE TO GET HOME.

Its rhythm is a sequence of DISCHARGES: the last bus at 22.30 leaving people
behind, the cinema at 22.50, the music room at 23.30. Everything the street
contains either HOLDS people before a discharge, which is a queue, a foyer or a
doorway, or ABSORBS them after one, which is hot food, a telephone or a cab.

That is a nightlife identity made of PAVEMENT rather than of rooms, and for
this game in particular it is better than a street of pubs. A pub's information
is inside a room the player has to enter and be seen entering. A queue's
information is in the open, at head height, where a player walks past it
without a door opening.

## 3. What is on the street, and why each thing is there

Nine units, in `data/parade-night.json`, all new fiction, no real business.
Every one is written as SEEN FROM THE STREET.

| unit | what it is for on this street |
|---|---|
| THE TIVOLI | the anchor and the clock. A 1928 picture house twinned in 1974 and cut into three in 1983, with a canopy carrying 24 lamps of which 19 work. It discharges two hundred people in ninety seconds at 22.50. |
| COLEY'S | the absorber. A chip shop is where a discharged crowd goes, and its queue is the longest static gathering of strangers on the street. |
| THE VIDEO SHOP | the reason to be on this street on a Tuesday. Friday-night rental is the actual British night ritual of 1990 and it needs no drink at all. |
| THE INSTITUTE HALL | a second, later, smaller discharge at 23.30, and a doorway that people keep coming out of to smoke. INTERIOR DEFERRED. |
| THE ANCHOR CAFE | the only lit window with people in it at a quarter past midnight. Drivers, shift workers, nurses. |
| THE PENNY ROOMS | the only MOVING light on the street: upright video cabinets seen through an open shopfront. No prize machine of any kind, which is a D17 decision and not a period claim. |
| THE NIGHT CHEMIST | one shut shop with a reason to knock on it. A rota plate and a night bell. |
| THE BRIGHT SPOT | the quiet information venue. A launderette enforces forty minutes of waiting on people who do not know each other, and it costs nothing. |
| TIDELINE RECORDS | shut by six, and it matters at night as a DARK unit between two lit ones, which is what makes the lit ones read. Named for canon's own pirate station. |

### The light is the design, and this is the argument

TAKE THE BREWERY SIGNS OUT OF A 1990 NIGHTLIFE STREET AND HALF ITS ILLUMINATED
SIGNAGE GOES WITH THEM. What is left is FOOD AND FILM: fluorescent tubes behind
shop glass, a cinema canopy, one small neon, sodium overhead.

That is a BETTER lighting brief than a row of internally lit drink signs, not a
worse one, and here is why in terms this project already uses. Canon's visual
target is wet, overcast and grimy. Internally lit box signs are flat, coloured
and self-contained: they light themselves and nothing else. A chip shop window
at full fluorescent throws a hard white pool three metres out across wet
flagstones, and a cinema canopy throws warm light down onto people's shoulders.
D17 REMOVES THE FLAT LIGHT AND LEAVES THE SPILL, and spill on wet stone is the
whole of what this game's exteriors are for.

Measured on the drawing: 7 of the 9 units are lit at 22.50.

### The day before it, which the atlas asked for and nobody had written

The fifth clause of the atlas row is "daytime cleaning and deliveries precede
night queues", and it had no material until now. A nightlife street in daylight
is a DIFFERENT STREET and the game gets two for one: in the morning the Parade
is a service yard with a pavement. Nobody queues, everything is open at the
back, and every door that will be locked at midnight is propped open with a
crate at nine. THAT IS THE ONLY HOUR A STRANGER ON THIS STREET IS
UNREMARKABLE, and it is when a player can walk into places.

Eight authored events, in the data file and drawn on the clock sheet. The one
that matters most: a cash collection from the amusements, in a bag, by two men,
at the same time every week. THE STREET'S ONE PIECE OF DAYLIGHT INFORMATION
WORTH HAVING.

## 4. The rank at midnight, which joins the two halves of this commission

A CAB OFFICE AND A NIGHTLIFE STREET ARE ONE ECONOMY. The atlas already names
taxi calls as part of this district and already lists a taxi landline notice
among its four objects. The Parade is where the fares are; Quay Street is where
the book is.

Four cars nose to tail outside the Tivoli from half past ten, on the double
yellows, engines running for the heater. Not a hackney rank: private hire cars
standing where the trade is, which is the same arrangement and the same small
illegality as the rank on Quay Street.

WHY A RANK IS AN INFORMATION PLACE, and this is the strongest single idea in
this file. A rank at midnight is one of the very few places in a town where
STRANGERS STAND STILL TOGETHER long enough to overhear each other. A bus queue
disperses when the bus comes. A cab queue argues about whose car is whose, in
the wet, for ten minutes at a time. AND THE DRIVER HEARS ALL OF IT, remembers
it, and takes it back to a room with a book in it. That is the moat working on
a street instead of in a room.

THE OBJECT THAT JOINS THEM is a direct line telephone in a steel hood screwed
to the wall between the cinema and the chip shop, with a plain face where a
dial would be. Lift it and it rings one office. Every fare on this street
begins with somebody lifting that handset in front of whoever is queueing
behind them and saying an address out loud.

  must not be the thing that hides it.

### The spec was validated against the tool, and the tool refused it first

Run against `tools/imagegen/imagegen.py`'s own functions, 2026-09-10:

- FIRST RUN: `validate problems=2/1itemsExamined`. Two refusals, both correct.
  The positive prompt contained the word "no" three times ("no dial", "no
  moon", "nobody talking"), which the tool bars because a diffusion model reads
  the noun and draws it. And the NEGATIVE half named four banned nouns, which
  the tool bars from every frame including the veto half:
  `forbiddenTokenHits=4/107tokens`.
- BOTH FIXED. "no dial on it" became "its face plain where a dial would be";
  "no moon" became "moonless"; "nobody talking" became "silent, each of them
  facing the window"; the four nouns came out of the negative entirely.
- SECOND RUN: `validate problems=0/1itemsExamined`,
  `posExclusionHits=0/628wordsScanned`, `negExclusionHits=0`,
  `forbiddenTokenHits=0/107tokens`, `negativeActive=False`, and the tool prints
  its own reason: at cfg 1.0 sd-cli never evaluates the unconditional branch,
  so the negative was not encoded and changed nothing. Estimated 152 seconds.

THE CONSEQUENCE OF THAT LAST LINE IS THE WHOLE ANTI-DRINK STRATEGY OF THIS
SPEC. A negative vetoes and cannot summon, and at cfg 1.0 it is not evaluated
at all, so D17 cannot be enforced from the veto half. It is enforced by a
POSITIVE clause instead, which reads: "everything on every sign and in every
window is food, film, music, laundry, medicine or a telephone number", and by
naming all nine units so the model has nothing left to fill the row with.

TWO WARNINGS THE RESIDENT NEEDS BEFORE RUNNING IT. First, the content rules
were copied from `tools/imagegen/prompts.json` BY SCRIPT at a recorded sha, and
that file was being edited by another agent at the moment of copying: it
carried 107 forbidden tokens where the Fairview sheet of the same day recorded
45. RE-VERIFY EQUALITY BEFORE RUNNING. Second, the tool's refusal message names
the rule as D18 while the decision register on disk names it D17. One of the
two is stale and it is not this commission's to decide which.

## 6. What this file does not decide

- NOTHING IS MINTED. Nine unit names, one hall and one cafe are PROPOSALS for
  the brand bible. Only Radio Tideline, on a sticker in a dark window, is a
  canon brand.
- NO GEOMETRY. D13 governs how the Parade is drawn and no coordinate is
  authored here. This is an identity and a frontage sequence, not a layout.
- NOT THE INTERIORS. See section 0.
- NOT WHETHER A PRIZE MACHINE MAY EVER APPEAR. The Penny Rooms contains none,
  which is the safe reading of D17, and the question is flagged rather than
  answered.
