## Coverage and gaps

The evidence now supports mixed-age commercial fabric, occupied post-war housing, continued cargo beside decline, non-universal municipal furniture, pub refitting, constrained pub service work and a hilly-port comparison. Meridian's particular cargo mix, employers, property histories, incomes and clocks remain authored fiction in the bible and atlas data.

The [Grimsby Fish archive record](https://www.yfanefa.com/record/7704) (1973–74, YFA3888) was read, but the film was **not watched**: no frame observations or 1990 handling claim is made. London Drinker 1988 vol10/10 was downloaded and contact sheets generated; no detailed observations from its unread scan are adopted. The council-hosted Barbican PDF was unavailable; the identified council-authored mirror was read instead. No 1990 directory, household inventory, transport timetable or pub operator interview has been obtained. Upper-floor possessions, exact keg/cask fittings, toilets, kitchen details and clothing across adult occupations need deeper source work before final art. The next useful research is a period small-pub measured plan/beer supplier catalogue and a dated hillside housing series, not more generic waterfront mood boards.

## D13 comparisons without false precision

Plymouth's published 10m/30m vertical differences are source estimates, not our survey. Meridian's 4m source-street datum and 45m upper contour are fictional. The source street's 6m road + two2m pavements give 10m nominal pedestrian-to-pedestrian cross-section; facade planes are 10.25m apart because source kerb offsets are retained. A 6.2m eaves height yields H/W ≈0.60, calculated from authored inputs. The 6×8m source bay has depth/frontage=1.33. These calculations are not D13 acceptance statistics.

Barbican Fig7 prints 1:4000, but the scanned publication's reproduction scale was not independently calibrated; no millimetre street-width claim is extracted from it. It supports contrasting passage/court/arterial morphology. A calibrated plan or georeferenced historic survey is still needed for reference width distributions, setbacks and block-depth comparison. Photographs have not been converted into precise measurements.
