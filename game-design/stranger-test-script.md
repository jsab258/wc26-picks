# The stranger test: what to say, and what to write down

> **STATUS: LIVE, verified 2026-09-06.** The asking script for
> production/queue/119. One page, read before the first person sits down.
> If a sentence here is wrong, that is a bug in this file.

You are the instrument. The program only starts the game; everything that
this test measures comes out of somebody's mouth after they have finished,
and the two ways to lose it are to tell them what to look for and to write
down what you think they meant instead of what they said.

## Before anybody arrives (ten minutes, once)

1. Double-click `RUN THE STRANGER TEST.bat` in the LEDGER folder. Type
   `1` and `1`. Play the whole thing yourself so the first build is done
   and so you know what they are about to see.
2. Delete `production\stranger-test\runs.txt` afterwards, or keep it and
   tell me which row was your practice one. Either is fine; a practice
   row counted as a person's is not.
3. Decide the three participant numbers now, in the order people are
   going to sit down, before you have any opinion about who they are.
   Do not save anybody for a particular number.

## What you say when you sit them down

Say roughly this, in your own words, and then stop talking.

> I have got a small thing I have been building. It takes about ten
> minutes and you play it by typing numbers. I would like you to play it
> twice, because nobody's first go at anything is about the thing, it is
> about working out the buttons. Then I will ask you a couple of
> questions. There is no right way to play it and nothing you can break.

Then: `RUN THE STRANGER TEST.bat`, their number, `1`. When it ends, run
it again with their number and `2`.

That is the whole setup. Do not say what the two runs are, do not say
whether they are the same, and do not say what you are hoping to find
out. If they ask straight out whether the two are different, say you
would rather not say until afterwards, and mean it.

## While they play

Sit where you cannot read the screen, or leave the room. If they ask you
what they should choose, say it is up to them. If they ask what something
means, say whatever they think it means is right. If they talk to
themselves, do not answer, and do not laugh at the good bits.

Say nothing at all between the first run and the second. Not "right,
again", not "now the other one". Start it and sit back down.

## The three questions, after BOTH runs, in this order

Ask them one at a time. After each one, stop, and let the silence do the
work. The thing you want is almost always the second thing somebody says,
after the polite first thing.

1. **"So what happened?"**
   Nothing else. Let them tell the story of it. Write down everything
   they say, in their words. THIS IS THE ONE THAT COUNTS: if the town
   knowing what they did comes up here, unprompted, that is the reading.
   If it does not come up here, it did not happen on its own.

2. **"Which of the two would you rather keep playing, and why?"**
   The "why" is the whole question. Let them ramble. If they say the two
   were the same, write that down exactly and go to question 3 anyway.

3. **"Did either of them feel more like a place with people in it?"**
   Last, and only last, because it names the thing. Asked earlier it
   would put the answer in their mouth. "No" and "they were the same"
   are real answers and they get written down word for word.

Then you can tell them what it was about, if you want to. Not before.

## What to write down

Their words. Not your summary of their words. If somebody says "the
second one felt like it noticed me", that sentence is the finding and
"participant preferred run 2" is not. If somebody says "I couldn't tell
them apart", that is the most important sentence anybody says all
evening and it goes down exactly like that.

For each person write: their number, what they said to question 1, what
they said to question 2, what they said to question 3, and anything they
said out loud while playing.

## Sending it back

Three things:

- what the three of them said, in their words
- `production\stranger-test\runs.txt`
- the folder `production\stranger-test\sessions`

The two files are what the program wrote down about itself. They do not
say who was right about anything and there is no score in them.

## How long the evening is

About thirty minutes a person: ten, ten, and ten of talking. Three people
with a break between them is a bit under two hours. It does not have to
be one evening and it does not have to be three people who know each
other.

## The one rule about the result

It gets reported whichever way it comes out. If all three cannot tell the
difference, that is the most useful thing this project has ever measured
and it goes in exactly as it landed. Nobody re-runs it with different
people until it comes out better.
