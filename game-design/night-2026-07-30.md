## Done

**Voice audit item 7 — speech has a bus.** `Core/Mixing` has described
`Bus.Voice` since the day it was written: a fourteen-metre reach, a budget of
four, a duck depth of zero because it is the thing everything else ducks
*for*, and a rule protecting an authored line from losing its slot to a
footstep. None of it was connected to an AudioSource. Speech in this game was
text in a bubble. There is now a Voice source, its own fader in the options
(it rode on the sfx slider, so turning footsteps down took the dialogue with
them), and distance driving both attenuation and the low-pass.

**Item 2 — the telephone has a sound of its own.** `PhoneBook` modelled the
social half — how much less you learn down a wire. The acoustic half did not
exist at all: a voice on the phone was sample-identical to the same voice in
the room, which throws away the identity of a mechanic with its own
milestone. Now: the ITU 300–3400 passband, a handset resonance baked into a
synthesised line bed, four line kinds, and two things that are mechanics
rather than decoration —

- **you cannot place a voice on a bad line.** Band-limiting removes exactly
  the cues used to recognise somebody, which is why anonymous calls work in
  every crime story ever written, and here it is free.
- **the caller's room comes down the wire.** A hall behind Ellis tells you
  which building he is standing in, and nobody wrote a line of dialogue for
  it.

A good handset is deliberately *above* the word-dropping threshold. Honest
physics says every call should be less intelligible than a face; the result
would be that every line of a core mechanic arrives with a hole in it, and a
mechanic that is annoying every single time is not a mechanic. The
degradation is spent on the callbox, the trunk and the bad junction instead.

**Item 5 — determinism.** A cloner given the same reference and text can
produce a different take, so a regenerated bank means every character drifts.
Clips are now named by a hash of who is speaking and what they say, and the
generator seeds from the same hash. The Python side computes the same names,
pinned by four shared test vectors asserted in CoreTests too — including one
emoji, specifically because that is the case where a naive port disagrees
with C# about what a character is and disagrees *silently*.

**Item 4 — captions, which are not subtitles.** `weapons-spec.md` §6.2 gives
four redundant channels telling you that you have been noticed and calls the
redundancy the point. Three of them are audio. "Subtitles-first" renders
exactly one, because subtitles are for what was *said* and nobody subtitles a
room going silent. So for a deaf player the four channels were one, and a
spec I wrote calling itself redundant was redundant for hearing players.

Every caption carries a direction in eight arcs (four would make "behind"
span a hundred and eighty degrees — the difference between turning round and
turning the *right* way) and how loud it was, because the noise ring exists
precisely because loudness is a mechanic. Not everything is captioned: the
threshold is the masking rule the game already uses, so at 3am a dropped
bottle is a caption and at noon it is not, which is also true of hearing it.

**macOS builds.** 98 MB `.app`, verified executable, artifact uploaded. It had
never once been compiled. No platform `#if` anywhere, no `.exe` assumption,
nothing in the way — which is exactly the shape of "should work" this project
keeps catching itself saying, so now a job says it instead.

## The three things that went wrong

**1. `breakrun.py` had been lying, and only a two-file spec could show it.**

It reverted the file the *next* break touched and left the others broken, so
break N stayed applied while break N+1 ran and the reported failure belonged
to the previous defect. Every spec until tonight was single-file, so it never
surfaced.

It reads as cosmetic mislabelling and it is not: a defect still sitting in the
tree can be caught by the previous round's check instead of its own, which
turns a SURVIVED into a RED — **the instrument claiming coverage it does not
have, inside the harness whose only job is to prove coverage is real.** Third
time this month the instrument was the thing at fault.

It paid for itself in one run. With it fixed, a break that drops the good
handset below the elision threshold *survived* — the check tested a single
seed and drew a lucky one. And `breaks/notice.json`, the only other two-file
spec, turned out to have two stale anchors that had not matched anything in
some time while the tally quietly counted them as survivors.

**2. The caption bar was dead on arrival and looked wired.**

Two of its three channels are *polled* in `Update` rather than pushed, and the
only thing that created the object was `Show()`. A hush could never have
appeared. Exactly the noise ring's failure — a system built, plausible, and
never once running — caught this time before the build rather than after four
of them.

**3. I asserted a run history I had not checked.**

A commit message said three voice runs were killed by the job cap. Two of them
I had cancelled myself, and the third was still running. I read a stale clock
and turned it into a confident sentence. Corrected in a follow-up commit
rather than quietly amended, because the correction is the useful artefact.

I also claimed a crowd pool of twenty-four voices with a confident argument
about the ear noticing repeats. The casting sheet funds **six**. Twenty-four
would have named files nobody was ever going to generate.

## Open, and yours

1. **Non-verbal voice — grunts, pain, exertion.** A cloner cannot make these
   at all. Phase 3 combat is a puppet show without them and it has lead time.
   *Recommendation: buy a human-effort foley library, ~$30–80.* Your account.
2. **Is cloning a donor's voice inside the consent rule?** *Recommendation:
   yes* — but yours to say explicitly rather than mine to keep assuming.
3. **Copper Row's character** — open since 27 July, blocking a re-cut.
4. **50 Hz or 60 Hz** mains hum on the telephone. *Recommendation: 50.* It is
   a ten-second change.

And two that are casting work rather than decisions:

- **The crowd pool is six voices.** Thin for a street — the ear picks a repeat
  out inside a minute. The fix is more crowd entries, not a larger constant.
- **The casting sheet and the game roster use different names.** `kest` vs
  `sera`, and several gossipers nobody has cast. An unknown id falls through
  to a crowd voice rather than throwing, so the symptom is a named character
  quietly sounding like a passer-by and nothing reporting it.

## Phase 2, and why it is not finished

`Witnesses` now turns a real street into the `Vantage` that `Observe.Resolve`
needs — position, facing, light on the actor as distinct from light on the
victim, walls. The delivery window is built: intercept before they arrive and
the mill is untouched; a minute later and it is indelible.

**The sequencing problem.** All five of §4.7's gate claims are about
witnessing a violent *deed*, and no deed can be performed until Phase 3 puts a
weapon on a button. So Phase 2 cannot be closed by a player action. The run
stages a synthetic deed instead — legitimate, because the deed is synthetic
and every number the witnesses are judged on is the live world, which is
exactly the half CoreTests cannot reach.

`deedSlotSets` is **reported and not yet gated**, deliberately. I have never
seen the number a real street produces, and a threshold chosen before the
first reading is a guess wearing a gate's clothes.

Still to do: routing witnesses to a destination on the real map, alarm
propagation, and the ghost.
