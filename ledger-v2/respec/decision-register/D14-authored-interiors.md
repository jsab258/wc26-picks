# D14: Authored interiors, and the stylised ceiling retired
Date: 2026-09-08. Status: APPROVED (Jafar, in session). Owner: Direction.
Context: two things in the written record contradicted what the project is now
building. First, scope-v2.md line 6 splits interiors into three tiers, of which
Tier 2 is "50 to 150 procedural interiors from a modular room grammar,
persisted as seed plus delta", with an experiment behind a perf gate:
"promotion by attention (any door forceable, Tier 2 interior generated on first
entry, delta kept forever)". Second, game-design/town-plan.md still sets the
register ceiling as "top-tier stylised, not photoreal", which D8 retired for
the exterior on 2026-08-31 and which nothing had retired for the interior.
Choice, in Jafar's words: "Every interior in scope is a designed layout with
chosen contents, assembled by scripts from designed kits. Scope-v2's procedural
Tier 2 room grammar is retired, and promotion by attention may generate nothing
that was not designed. The stylised ceiling in game-design/town-plan.md is
retired. Canon and D8 govern: photoreal late-analog Britain. Keep the plan's
urban observations."
What this does and does not retire, stated so no future session has to infer it.
RETIRED: the room grammar as a generator of layouts, and any path by which a
door opened for the first time produces a room nobody designed. ASSEMBLY BY
SCRIPT SURVIVES and is the method: a script places designed kit pieces into a
designed layout, which is exactly how the street's 593 pieces are built today.
The distinction is authorship, not automation. A script that assembles a
designed room is in scope; a grammar that invents one is not.
"IN SCOPE" IS THE LOAD-BEARING PHRASE. This decision does not set how many
interiors exist. It says that whatever the number is, each one is designed.
Tier 3's fake-interior shader for a window nobody enters is untouched: it is
not an interior, it is a texture on glass.
Consequence for promotion by attention: it may still open a door that was
previously closed, and it may still persist what the player does inside for
ever. It may not GENERATE. An unentered door either leads to a designed
interior or is not forceable.
Consequence for the town plan: the sentence "The register ceiling is top-tier
stylised, not photoreal" is dead, and so is "Style stays cheap on purpose". The
plan's urban observations are kept and are the reason it is not simply deleted:
enclosure, hierarchy, grammar, purpose, and "nothing in shot that a town would
not have put there" are about how a town is ORGANISED, which no change of
visual bar touches.
Instrument: nothing new. The Meridian Test conditions 1 and 3 already judge
whether a player bounces off the visuals and whether the town reads as alive,
and an interior is in shot when they do.
Revisit when: never, absent a gate failure that names this decision.
