# D11: player progression

STATUS: DECIDED by Jafar in the planning session. BACKFILLED 2026-09-02 from
his dictation, keeping the number so D12's judgment-legibility citation
resolves. The planning session issued this decision and the paste was never
delivered, so the number was live in citations before any file carried it.

## The decision

**The player character has no improvable stats or skills.** The v1 stance
stands. Nothing about the player gets numerically better at anything.

**Progression is EXTERNAL, and it is threefold.**

### 1. Being believed, emergent and per person

Each NPC weighs a claim against THEIR OWN remembered record of the player:
promises kept, lies caught, alibis that checked out.

**Never a stored global credibility number**, per the reference-extraction
reject list. There is no "reputation: 47" anywhere. Being caught in a lie
damages that record WITH THE WITNESS, and spreads through the mill like any
other fact.

### 2. The player's own knowledge of the town

Schedules, relationships, unwatched routes. Earned by play, and STORED
NOWHERE. It lives in the person holding the controller.

### 3. Crew, relationships and access improve while the player does not

The world changes around a constant protagonist.

## Legibility, per the psychic-guard lesson

When a claim is believed or doubted, THE REASON MUST BE SURFACEABLE from the
NPC's relevant memory. A judgement the player cannot trace to something that
actually happened reads as the game deciding arbitrarily, which is the
psychic-guard failure in a new costume.

## Why the shape is what it is

A global credibility number would be cheap to build and would quietly delete
the moat. It makes lying a resource-management problem against one meter,
readable at a glance, identical everywhere. Per-person records make the same
lie cost different amounts to different people, make WHO SAW IT matter, and
give the gossip mill something real to carry. The reject list named the
global number for that reason and this decision holds the line.

The player having no skills is the same argument from the other end: if the
player improves, the interesting question becomes "what have I unlocked".
If only the world's model of the player improves, the interesting question
stays "what do they think of me, and who told them", which is the game.

## What this does not decide

The Ledger's presentation of any of it, which is D12. Whether crew capability
is a number or a roster, which is systems work. The rate at which the mill
carries a caught lie, which needs a measured series and has none.
