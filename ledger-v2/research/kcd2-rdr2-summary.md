# KCD2 and RDR2 teardown, condensed (full report: research/full/, Jafar exports)

The finding: what made both worlds alive splits into transferable systems logic (cheap) and non-transferable content mass (the arbitrage).

THE NUMBERS (developer-stated, not audited)
RDR2: 500,000 recorded lines, 1,200 actors (700 speaking), 2,200 mocap days, about 300,000 animations, some ambient NPCs with 80-page scripts. KCD2: 2.2 million-word script (about 25 novels), the lead actor alone 508 booth hours across 127 sessions, about 250 staff, 40 to 45 million USD, five and a half years. Both used zero generative AI. Most of that spend bought dialogue variety and reactive lines, exactly what the pipeline generates at near-zero marginal cost. The spend that cannot be arbitraged: animation and physics fidelity.

BOTH GAMES FAKE PERSISTENCE
KCD2 deletes known felonies when punishment is accepted; wanted status expires in days. RDR2 witnesses exist in short-lived bubbles; shopkeeper memory is a scripted flag. Permanent per-NPC memory plus gossip is the thing neither attempted.

ADOPT FROM KCD2
1. AI LOD: about 2,400 NPCs at a 60fps target by fully simulating only who is near; coarse schedule ticks elsewhere. Set limits early.
2. Witness-and-deduction crime (a resident near the scene suffices; unwitnessed murder can go unpunished) and conspicuousness-informed identification. LEDGER's slots and ladder are the deeper version; keep the legibility.
3. Witness choice: report or blackmail the player for silence.
4. Punishment escalation ending in a visible social mark (their branding halves reputation gains and changes who will deal with you). LEDGER version: never a wipe, remediation is behavioral.
5. Needs and friction: food, sleep, dirt, clothing-as-status, degradation. Speech checks read appearance.
6. Time-sensitive and self-resolving quests (the world proceeds without you).
7. The two-horses rule (already in the constitution).

ADOPT FROM RDR2
1. Witness runs to report; intercept, threaten, bribe or kill; killing can mint a new witness (chains). Era version: the run is to a phone box or the station, a built-in timer smartphones cannot give.
2. Camp as social hub: morale and contribution counters plus proximity chatter that references recent events. Counters transfer; the chatter is generated, memory-conditioned.
3. Random-encounter skeletons with trigger conditions; generation fills variation to fight repetition.
4. Moving world furniture on schedules (their trains; our buses, milk floats, brewery drays).
5. Newspapers reporting player deeds (the local paper is already in the design).

REJECT
Honor-style global morality scalar (self-correcting, shallow; reactions derive from what individuals witnessed and heard). Euphoria-class physics and animation parity (even Rockstar shows seams: insta-skin on slopes). Forced save friction as default.

FAILURE MODES TO DESIGN AGAINST
1. Psychic guards (KCD2's top complaint): deep simulation without legibility feels unfair; the what-they-know HUD is the antidote.
2. Hollow generated people (Watch Dogs Legion): authored breadth is the deliberate opposite.
3. Overpromised simulation (STALKER 2 A-Life shipped as a spawn bubble and the community caught it in days): never claim what cannot be demonstrated.
4. The repetition tell: both games reveal their loops over long playthroughs; non-repetition is the visible bar where LEDGER can beat both, and the blind test in Phase 3 measures it.

THRESHOLD RULE
If performance ever forces a choice between more people and real memory, cut the people. A small town where everyone truly remembers beats a big city that forgets.
